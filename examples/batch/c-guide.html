Reading a weather radar image

A radar image shows the reflected energy of precipitation. Brighter colours mean heavier rain or hail, and the colour scale is printed beside the map.

Echoes that appear in a ring around the station are often ground clutter or beam blockage. Compare several scans before deciding that a storm is really there.

Animated loops show the direction and speed of movement. A cell that grows and moves steadily is more likely to produce a strong storm than one that stays put.

Radar cannot see below the beam at long range, so light rain far from the station may be missed entirely.
