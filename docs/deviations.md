# Deviations from upstream

Every place this crate departs from upstream Python trafilatura at the pinned release, deliberate or
forced. The code carries `// deviation: D-n` at the spot. A row leaves when the difference is gone,
for example after upstream changed; ids are never reused.

| Id | Upstream behavior | This crate | Reason | Since |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D-1 | `fast=False` by default: readability and jusText run after the main extractor (`external.py`) and can replace its result | Fast mode only: no `fast` option, no readability or jusText, every `options.fast` branch takes the fast side | Keeps the first releases thin; a caller that needs a fallback runs one on a rejection. Porting `fast=False` is Phase 11 | first release |
| D-2 | `lxml.html` parses with libxml2: HTML5 tokenizer, libxml2's own tree construction (no implied `tbody`, no adoption agency) | html5ever: HTML5 tokenizer and tree construction, into the crate's arena tree. | D-3 | Bytes that are not valid UTF-8 are decoded with `charset_normalizer`'s guesses (`utils.py::detect_encoding`); upstream drops a UTF-8 guess, so UTF-8 text with a few stray invalid bytes, where charset_normalizer returns no match, has no candidate and is decoded as UTF-8 with U+FFFD | `chardetng` makes one guess, decoded with `encoding_rs`. | D-5 | libxml2 has no element-count limit beyond nesting depth 256, and builds no reconstructed copies of formatting elements | The parse stops with `Rejection::TreeTooLarge(TreeLimit::MaxNodes)` once more than `DEFAULT_MAX_NODES` (500,000) elements are created, html5ever's clones of open formatting elements included; a caller sets `max_nodes: None` to lift it; a markup value of an embedded-JSON script (`baseline.py::_render_text`) whose fragment parse hits the limit falls back to the trimmed text, as upstream's `except` branch does for a fragment it cannot parse. A tag (start or end) with more than `MAX_TAG_ATTRIBUTES` (1,000) attributes, and an `html` or `body` element whose attributes merged from repeated tags pass that count, also give `Rejection::TreeTooLarge(TreeLimit::TagAttributes)`; a scan before html5ever reads the input checks the tags and counts duplicate names too, and a markup fragment these caps reject falls back to the trimmed text as for the node limit. Two budgets scale with `max_nodes` and are lifted with it: the attributes created in one parse, reconstructed clones included, may not pass 10 per allowed node (5,000,000 at the default), and html5ever's work on formatting elements may not pass 40 units per allowed node (20,000,000 at the default; one unit is 13 ns). The work is charged where html5ever spends it: once per formatting start tag, against each same-name formatting ancestor of the element the tag creates (the entries Noah's Ark compares are exactly those open elements; the clones of reconstruction and of the adoption agency run no compare and are not charged one) 12 units per pair plus, for each of the two attribute lists, a clone and a sort priced as a shuffled list (one unit per attribute, plus one per attribute and level of a merge sort, plus one per 128 bytes of names per level); 10 units per attribute of every formatting element created, which prices the clones of reconstruction; and once per end tag that html5ever answers with "Formatting element not in scope", the end tag that clones the element's attributes and leaves it in the list. A gate between tokenizer and tree builder drops every token once the budget is crossed, so html5ever's work after the abort is one token's, not the rest of its 8 KiB piece. The budget rejects, although html5ever would finish within about 0.3 s: a page that leaves many same-name formatting elements of distinct attributes open and reconstructs them in every paragraph (paragraphs of `<p><font color=cN size=2>t</p>` cycling 10 colors are rejected from 14,941 and parse at 2,000, cycling 20 colors from 7,550; with every color distinct the open elements reach the depth cut of 256 and the partial tree of 32,641 elements is kept at any length), a page of 95,240 or more paragraphs that each leave an identical `font` of three attributes open (a paragraph costs 210 units), and a page whose same-name formatting ancestors sit before a `td`, `th`, `caption`, `template`, `applet`, `object` or `marquee` marker, which html5ever stops comparing at but the charge still counts | html5ever's tree construction multiplies elements quadratically on a small page (102 KB gives 4.0M elements, peak about 1.3 GB), which would abort an FFI host; 500k is about 18 times the largest corpus page (27,741 elements), and a page of 499,000 paragraphs, just under the limit, peaks at 420 MB of heap in the whole extraction. html5ever's duplicate-attribute check compares each attribute with every earlier one of the same tag (quadratic: one tag of the size of the input cap would take hours), and no corpus tag comes near 100 attributes (the most is 38). The tree builder adds two more attribute costs no single tag triggers (release build, 1,000-attribute `<b>` elements): its Noah's Ark compare clones and sorts both attribute lists for every same-name entry (50 nested `<b>` then 2,000 `<b></b>` took 2.7 s for 259 KB, 200 nested and 2,000 took 11.7 s, a foster-parented and a misnested `</p>` variant 12.0 s and 11.7 s; with shuffled names the same page of 2,000 probes took 2.3 s at 10 MB, because the sorts then do their full work, and names of 70 bytes with a common prefix 2.8 s at 63 MB), reconstruction clones the attributes of every open entry (250 entries and 2,000 `<p>` made 500,000 clones and about 12 GB; 14 names of 14 entries of 1,000 attributes took 1.5 to 2.8 s before the budget tripped, values of 0 to 200 bytes), and 64 MiB of `</b>` after a heavy `<b>` under a `<table>` took 137 s (7 microseconds each, a clone of the attributes). With the budgets those pages end rejected in 0.06 to 0.37 s, in-order names, shuffled names with a 1,000 byte prefix and the reconstruction pages included, even at the 64 MiB input cap. A page the budget does not reject runs at html5ever's own speed, which no budget of this deviation bounds: 64 MiB of comments takes 2.0 to 2.3 s with or without heavy open formatting elements, and 64 MiB of `</x>` takes 2.1 to 2.2 s alone or behind one, 5.0 to 5.1 s behind 20 open formatting elements and 7.1 to 7.3 s behind 40, since each end tag walks the open elements (a heavy element costs the same as one without attributes); behind 250 it takes 28 s, a figure measured with attribute-free elements; the corpus maximum is 10,154 attributes and 62,920 units of work, 492 and 318 times below the budgets, and a page of 20,000 paragraphs with an unclosed `font` is charged 4,199,640 units (21 percent) | first release |
